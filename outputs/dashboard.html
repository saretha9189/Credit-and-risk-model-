Credit Risk Studio

A default-risk model for credit card applicants, drawn in three dimensions so you can see which people the model trusts and which it does not.

Drag any chart to turn it. Scroll to zoom.
Applicants

Every dot is a person

A random 1,800 of the held-out applicants. Switch between what actually happened and what the model predicted, and swap the axes to look from another angle.

Risk surface

Age, income and default risk

Each point is the model's average predicted default risk if every applicant had that age and income, holding the rest of their profile as it is. Move the slider to change how long they have lived at their address.

Approval cutoff

Where to draw the line

The curve is the model's ROC curve with the score threshold on the vertical axis. The pink marker is your cutoff: applicants scoring above it are declined.

Segments

Default rate by profession and occupation

Height is the share of applicants in that group who defaulted. The flat pink sheet marks the overall rate. Groups with fewer than 40 applicants are left out.

Drivers

What the model leans on

Three views of the same ten variables, each scaled so its tallest bar is 1: how much the boosting model loses when the variable is shuffled, the same test for the logistic scorecard, and information value.

    How well the scores sort people

    Held-out applicants, split into ten equal groups from lowest to highest predicted risk. The predicted and actual columns should rise together.
